Symmetry and Congruence

Grade 6 Mathematics · Explore, transform and compare shapes.

Your goal: recognize reflection and rotation symmetry, understand congruent shapes, and investigate how shapes make tessellations. You can retry every activity. Nothing is timed.

1. Reflection symmetry

A shape has reflection symmetry when one side is the mirror image of the other side across a line. The line is called a line of symmetry.

The dashed line divides the butterfly into matching mirror halves.

To test reflection symmetry, imagine folding the shape along the line. If both halves match exactly, it has reflection symmetry.

2. Rotation symmetry

A shape has rotation symmetry when it can be turned around a centre by an angle less than a full turn and still match its starting position.

A turn can bring a shape back to a matching position.

For example, a square matches itself after a quarter-turn (90°), a half-turn (180°) or three-quarter-turn (270°).

3. Congruent shapes

Two shapes are congruent when they have exactly the same shape and size. They can face different directions or be in different places.

One shape can be moved, reflected or rotated to line up exactly with the other. Congruence is not changed by position or orientation.

4. Tessellations

A tessellation is a pattern of shapes that covers a flat surface without gaps or overlaps. Symmetrical shapes can be used to make tessellations.

Look for tessellations in floor tiles, brickwork, art and architecture. The Alberta curriculum also asks students to investigate tessellations in First Nations and Métis star blanket designs and consider the specific purposes they convey.

Respectful learning: star blanket designs can carry cultural meaning and purpose. They are not simply decorative patterns. Learn about a design from appropriate First Nations or Métis sources rather than guessing what it means.
